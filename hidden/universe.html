Starboy Connected Universe

aka: All my faves know each other because I said so.

What is the Starboy Connected Universe?

The Starboy Connected Universe (SCU) is a world in which I drop all my favorite characters and play with them like they're my dolls. Eveybody knows everybody to varying degrees, and it's one of the most self-indulgent manifestations of my love for these characters.

Who's Who in the SCU?

Niou Masaharu

As my main starboy, Masaharu is usually at the center of the SCU as the main protagonist.

Masaharu's story isn't the most grounded of the three, but his Magical Tennis World serves as a bridge between Xavier's world of Evols and Luke's reality grounded in science. Tennis can be a hard magic world, and I can assign them seeds, or it can be something softer and more forgiving. Regardless, Masaharu is the star at the center of the SCU, and most stories revolve around him being the baby of the trio that the other two dote on. Tennis is Serious, and most characters in Masaharu's canon world move through the story like they're adults, but Masaharu turns fifteen in the sequel series. I often write him younger, and no matter how magical things are, the world is grounded enough to recognize children as children.

Masaharu is a baby, and he will be treated as such.

The adults in his life worry after him when he loses weight, and they encourage him to wear his ribbons and hair pins when he shows interest. His gender non-conformity is played straight, and his female illusions aren't dismissed along with the cat illusions. Instead, they're reflective of how he's trying to figure out who he is and what he wants. Everyone around him is so supportive and loving, always affirming him when he makes a change in his appearance. Sometimes, it's a new discovery, but oftentimes, Masaharu is returning to aesthetics he liked when he was younger, before society told him that boys had to act and look a certain way.

The societal expectations aren't just gender-oriented though.

In the SCU, Masaharu is usually masking like his life depends on it, and that's because he thinks it does. He's autistic, and it's not just in the fun, quirky way. He's disabled, and it's obvious to anyone who gets to know him. His family is incredibly supportive, but he's insecure because being the more obviously disabled kid in a family of more socially-acceptable disabled people will mess with you, no matter how accepting and accomodating your family is. Masaharu struggles with internalized ableism. He knows he can't do everything on his own, and he hates it. Usually, the SCU has him struggle to keep everything together until he simply can't anymore. It's messy, it's emotional, and it's usually the only way to get through to him that accepting help is okay.

As an aside, sometimes Masaharu has a fictional condition known as Foliastic Nous Disorder. Several character have it, but Masaharu is always on the more affected side of the spectrum. Sometimes, he's forced into being more baby through sheer genetics, but other times, Masaharu is more like his canon self. I've written several foliastic stories, but recently, I've come to like the ones where it's not a medical condition that makes him the way it is. Or rather, it's not a wide-spread condition.

Anyway, Masaharu is baby. He's the most adorable boy in existence, and the SCU is one of the ways I give him the space to be himself. If I exaggerate some of his traits to suit whatever tropes and storylines I'm craving at the moment, that's nobody's business but my own. I'll develop OCs later.

Shizume Seiya

Xavier joins the SCU under his Japanese name, Seiya, and he is assigned the last name Shizume, which is sourced from different readings of his surname when written in Mandarin, BLANK (read as Shen).

In the SCU, Seiya is Masaharu's maternal uncle, a younger cousin on his mother's side. Seiya is the heir to a (business) empire he wants nothing to do with because his father was not a good man. As far as he's concerned, the business can rot, but he reluctantly comes to terms with the fact that there are certain benefits to his privilige that he can use to his advantage.

Initially, Seiya is hiding in Japan. He was born Xavier, and Shizume is his mother's maiden name. Seiya was the name she chose for him, and it's his name on the Shizume family register. He hates his father's name, and he'll never use it.

He owns a museum about stars and space travel. It was his mother's dream, and she left it for him to complete before her passing. When he's not there, he's helping at Jeremiah's flower shop.

Minase Natsuhiko

Like Xavier, Luke joins the SCU under his Japanese name, KANJI, read as Minase Natsuhiko.

Natsuhiko still runs Times Antiquities and works as a private detective after leaving the NSB, but he moved to Tokyo from Stellis to start fresh. He has a past as an agent that he doesn't like to speak about.

Interesting, Natsuhiko's health issues and combat abilities make him a fun match for the main character of Love and Deepspace. As a result, he bridges the gap between the two otome games. Instead of being a love interest, he is the main character that lives near Seiya and is romanced by him.

Fun fact, Luke has the same Chinese surname as Caleb Xia, the Love and Deepspace love interest who grew up with MC as her adopted brother after meeting as orphans and being experimented on together. Caleb, who goes by Minase Mahiru in his SCU appearances, is an older relative whose family took him in after his parents passed. Mahiru's parents passed when he was in university, and this is what pushed Natshiko to join the NSB. He felt guilty when Mahiru considered dropping out, and he feels even worse when he gets sick and has to retire.

Rosa is still a school friend who works at a law firm, but we don't see her unless lawsuits are filed, and there's a Stellis Law firm reveal.

Favorite Storylines

The Prestige

My magnum opus, ngl.

This is a story which examines the various traumas associated with being an elite child athlete.

Currently, we're working on the first draft, but in terms of the SCU, Seiya and Natsuhiko don't have much of a role yet. Masaharu has an uncle on his dad's side that he's much closer to, but I'm considering bringing the Seiya Natsuhiko coupling in as Natshiko could have strong empathy for what Masaharu's going through. Maybe he had a bad experience with his superiors after getting sick, and that's the real reason he left.

I don't really want to traumatize him like that, but Luke's story does have elements of his body being used to further a goal re: Jerome's experimentation, so it could be an interesting way to bring him into the story in an alt-line.

Long Lost Son

Tentatively titled Little Star, How I Wonder Where You Are, affectionately nicknamed Little Star.

The story follows a fifteen year old Masaharu, currently going by Nishio Masatoshi due to being kidnapped and brainwashed by one Nishio Kanami. Masaharu knows this is not his real identity, but most adults think he's got a trauma-induced overactive imagination because Nishio Toshio did indeed go missing. However, that Nishio Toshio was not the one who was found.

In a bid to change his fate and get away from the woman he knows is not his mother, Masaharu applies to for a student internship at ODDYSSEI, one of the top architecture firms in the country. The firm provides tuition and a living stipend so the students can focus on being students, and it's the only way Masaharu will be able to afford to board at Yokaze Tech or Hyoutei Gakuen. Kanami refuses to let him stray too far from her home in Kanagawa, and he needs to get out.

Upon his arrival to orientation, Masaharu, going by his preferred name of Maa-kun, quickly finds himself in a situation where he suspectes that the ODDYSSEI CEO is part of his missing family. The only thing stopping him from saying anything is the knowledge that Niou Seishirou's family died in a plane crash a few years earlier. However, when one of his new boss's in-laws (Seiya) sees him wandering around the company, paternity tests are swiftly ordered.

Family ties confirmed, Masaharu rejoins the grieving Niou-Shizume family, and he gets closer to his father, his grandfather, his maternal uncle, Seiya, and Uncle Seiya's boyfriend/bodyguard, Natsuhiko. They are a close unit, but things have been tense since Reika and the kids passed. Masaharu's recovery heals something in the family and himself.

Sometimes, this is a world where there are powers, but currently, I don't want Masaharu to be under an Illusion when he meets his dad. As fun as it is for Grandpa Masaharu to be the one to cause a big reveal in the hospital... I don't know if the final version will have that. We'll see.

Long Lost Orphan

This is basically Little Light, but Seiya, Natsuhiko, and Kouhei raise Masaharu after he's found. Kouhei is the ODDYSSEI CEO because Seishirou passed away in the accident instead of him, and Masaru helps, but he lives in Takamatsu. Seiya and Kouhei are at odds with each other, and Natsuhiko is often the voice of reason. It's very Three Men and a Baby (I've never seen this show? Movie?).

This is an even more painful story because there comes a point where Masaharu cries and says he wants his dad, but his dad is dead, and he's not coming back. Seiya and Kouhei both see their lost family members in him, and they try not to, but it's very... yeah.

Post-Sabbatical (Extended Universe)

Canonically, Masaharu chooses to travel after the Junior World Cup to rediscover himself and his tennis. In the extended verse, he finds himself in Stellis and goes to an escape room. He signs up to be a filler member, and he's added to a room with Seiya, Natsuhiko, Mahiru, Kudou Shinichi from Detective Conan/, and Kuroba Kaito from Magic Kaito. It's a six-person room, and they all have a blast together. Seiya, Natsuhiko, and Mahiru came as a trio, Shinichi and Kaito came as a duo, and Masaharu was a singleton. They have an absurd age gap, but they seem like equals.

This changes when Mahiru treats everyone to snacks after, and a murder happens.

Natsuhiko and Shinichi solve the murder in record time, but before that, Masaharu kind of... glitches . He doesn't deal with stuff like that in his home story, so he doesn't know how to deal with it in this one. As a result, the older characters solidify in their minds that no matter how brilliant this fifteen-year-old is, he's still fifteen years old. He's been through stuff, but nothing like the others have. And they don't want him to deal with things, so Seiya draws his attention away with tactile stimulation, eventually leading him to a quieter corner of the cafe. Mahiru helps form a wall, blocking his line of sight, and Kaito does magic tricks to occupy his mind.

After the murder, they don't ditch Masaharu. They continue to look after him and engage with him. They're all from Japan in the end, so they agree to keep in touch. When Masaharu starts high school, they make sure to show up to support him. They baby him and look after him, especially in orphan-verse- adjacent storylines. Rikkai is perplexed, but Hyoutei support their relationship.

Masaharu and Seiya aren't related in this one, or they don't know they're related until much later if they are, so this truly is a found family story where everyone takes care of everyone because all of their stories are canon to a certain extent. NXX is related to Mahiru's experimentation is related to APTX is related to Pandora is related to Xavier's family seeking imortality.

How does Masaharu fit? He's normal to them until they realize he's from a family of assorted changelings that whoever is in charge was tracking. Masaharu being the family's first true changeling would have marked him for capture and experimentation in the same way Mahiru and Luke were experimented on had the criminal empire not been taken down. Tennis is not quite magic, but some of the tennis boys have magic. Some of them were seeds planted in the same way Luke and Mahiru were, and the U-17 tournament was supposed to be a harvest until certain Karasuma benefactors were shut down.

To the older men, Masaharu is a baby who needs to be protected, who they almost lost before they ever met. They can and will jump anyone who upsets their starlight, especially if it's one of the more magical tennis boys. One of my favorite scenes in these stories is when they all come to support his high school matches and are two seconds away from jumping his old team when they make snide comments.

NB: Kudou Shinichi and Kuroba Kaito, although they are members of the extended SCU, are not starboys. We won't be discussing them in depth on this page.
